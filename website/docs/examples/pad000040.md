# Obesity and diabetes (PAD000040)

Every step on real data: two raw plate files from a free public dataset, to quality-checked,
bridged data with its sample metadata. Every output is real.
{ .lede }

## 1. Get the data

**PAD000040** from the PRIDE archive (free to use, CC0): Olink Target 96 Inflammation, 92 proteins,
142 people with severe obesity, two plates, diabetes status. Download three files from
[PRIDE: PAD000040](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/pride/archive/projects/PAD000040) into one folder:

| File | What it is |
|---|---|
| `inflam_NASH_dt1.raw.csv` | plate 1, raw |
| `inflam_PRA_dt2.raw.csv` | plate 2, raw |
| `2025.05.08_sample_manifest.xlsx` | the sample list, with diabetes status |

<p class="small">Milito MM, …, Giraudi PJ. Inflammatory proteomic heterogeneity beyond glycemia
status in severe obesity. Int J Mol Sci 2026;27(9):4152.
<a href="https://doi.org/10.3390/ijms27094152">doi:10.3390/ijms27094152</a></p>

## 2. Read the two plates

```python
import pandas as pd
import signatomics.olink as so

plates = pd.concat([so.read_npx("inflam_NASH_dt1.raw.csv"),
                    so.read_npx("inflam_PRA_dt2.raw.csv")], ignore_index=True)
```

The reading report of plate 2:

## 3. Quality control

The diabetes status is in the sample list, so the quality check looks it up there:

```python
manifest = "2025.05.08_sample_manifest.xlsx"
so.qc_report(plates, group="diabetes", samples=manifest, on="FIF ID", show_ids=False)
```

```text
--8<-- "outputs/pad_qc_report.txt"
```

Two findings matter here. People with prediabetes were measured only on plate 2 (or on both), so
a plate difference would look like a diabetes difference: bridge before comparing.

```python
clean = so.apply_qc(plates, show_ids=False)
```

```text
--8<-- "outputs/pad_apply_qc.txt"
```

Every protein stays unless you give a threshold (`max_below_lod=`): a protein undetectable in
one group and measurable in another can itself be a biomarker. To remove the odd measurement as
well, see [Quality control](../learn/quality-control.md).

## 4. Bridging

```python
so.bridge_check(clean, group="diabetes", samples=manifest, on="FIF ID", show_ids=False)
```

```text
--8<-- "outputs/pad_bridge_check.txt"
```

Plate 2 differs from plate 1 by −0.96 to +2.30 NPX depending on the protein, so each protein gets
its own correction. Overall the shift is the same in every diabetes group. Six proteins shift
differently between groups, each measured on only 9 or 10 bridge samples per group: look at
those before trusting a diabetes difference in them.

<div class="sg-plot" data-src="../../assets/figures/bridge_scatter.json"><img src="../../assets/figures/bridge_scatter.svg" alt="Three panels: bridge samples, NPX on plate 1 against plate 2, for DNER, TNFRSF9 and ST1A1" loading="lazy"></div>
<figcaption markdown>`so.plot_bridge_scatter(clean, ["DNER", "TNFRSF9", "ST1A1"])`. On the dashed diagonal both plates agree; dotted lines: the LOD per plate. Plate 2 reads DNER 2.3 NPX higher, TNFRSF9 the same, and ST1A1 about 1 NPX lower. The dot at the bottom of each panel is sample 249, the broken tube.</figcaption>
</figure>

How bridging corrects this, for one protein:

--8<-- "bridging.html"

```python
bridged = so.bridge_plates(clean)
so.plot_bridge_pca(clean, bridged)
```

<figure class="sg-chart" markdown>
<div class="sg-plot" data-src="../../assets/figures/bridge_pca.json"><img src="../../assets/figures/bridge_pca.svg" alt="Two PCA panels of the bridge samples coloured by plate, before and after bridging" loading="lazy"></div>
<figcaption markdown>`so.plot_bridge_pca(clean, bridged)`. Each line joins one sample's two measurements; the point far from the rest in both panels is sample 249.</figcaption>

Before bridging, a sample's two measurements lie 6.5 apart in the PCA (median); after, 1.8, and
the plates overlap. The PCA is the independent check: `bridge_plates` fits one shift per protein,
so per-protein checks look perfect by construction, while the PCA looks at all proteins together.


## 6. Analysis table

For the statistics, run the route once more from the plates and remove sample 249 on plate 2, the
broken tube from the bridging step, before bridging. Then make the analysis table: one row per
person, one column per protein. The bridge samples were measured twice; `to_wide` averages their two
values.

```python
data = so.add_sample_info(plates, manifest, on="FIF ID")
clean = so.apply_qc(data, drop_samples={"249": "Inflammation PRAESIIDIUM Giraudi 04_03_2025"})
table = so.to_wide(so.bridge_plates(clean))
```

```text
--8<-- "outputs/pad_to_wide.txt"
```

## 7. Compare the groups

The groups run from no diabetes through prediabetes to type 2 diabetes (T2DM), so one trend test
asks the sharpest question: does a protein rise step by step?

```python
from signatomics.stats import compare_groups, plot_volcano

order = ["No diabetes", "Prediabetes", "T2DM"]
trend = compare_groups(table, "diabetes", reference="No diabetes", ordered=True, order=order)
```

```text
--8<-- "outputs/pad_trend.txt"
```

IL8, Flt3L and CDCP1 rise by 0.2 to 0.3 NPX per step, so T2DM sits about 0.5 NPX (1.4 times) above
no diabetes.

<figure class="sg-chart" markdown>
<div class="sg-plot" data-src="../../assets/figures/volcano_trend.json"></div>
<figcaption markdown>`plot_volcano(trend)`. Each dot is one protein; the three coloured dots, above the dotted line, pass the FDR of 0.05.</figcaption>
</figure>

Without an order, `compare_groups(table, "diabetes", reference="No diabetes")` runs Welch's ANOVA, which
accepts any pattern and finds no protein at an FDR of 0.05 (lowest FDR 0.09). The trend test puts its
power on one pattern. The choice between them is made before looking at the results. The pairs in
`.pairwise` show that T2DM differs from no diabetes for all three proteins (Holm p of 0.001 to
0.004), with prediabetes in between.

## 8. Adjust for covariates

The sample list holds one covariate, sex, and it is unevenly spread: 62 of the 69 people without
diabetes are women, against 26 of the 48 with T2DM. `covariates=["sex"]` fits `NPX ~ diabetes + sex`
for every protein, with the same model for all of them.

```python
from signatomics.stats import plot_volcano_pair

two = table[table["diabetes"].isin(["No diabetes", "T2DM"])]
unadjusted = compare_groups(two, "diabetes", reference="No diabetes")
adjusted = compare_groups(two, "diabetes", reference="No diabetes", covariates=["sex"])
plot_volcano_pair(unadjusted, adjusted)
```

<figure class="sg-chart" markdown>
<div class="sg-plot" data-src="../../assets/figures/volcano_pair.json"></div>
<figcaption markdown>`plot_volcano_pair(unadjusted, adjusted)`. Left: three proteins pass the FDR. Right: after adjusting for sex none does, and the direction stays the same.</figcaption>
</figure>

Unadjusted, Flt3L, IL8 and CDCP1 pass an FDR of 0.05. After adjusting for sex the differences shrink
by 18 to 32% (IL8 from +0.60 to +0.40 NPX) and the intervals widen, and no protein passes. Only 7 people
without diabetes are men, so the data separate "T2DM" from "male" poorly. What the data support: a
difference of about +0.4 to +0.6 NPX with T2DM, and this study is too small to say how much of it sex
explains. A sample list with age, BMI and medication would give a fuller adjusted analysis with the
same two lines.

## 9. Find a signature

Can a few proteins tell T2DM from no diabetes in people the model has not seen? Sex goes into
every model, so the report also shows what sex alone predicts.

```python
from signatomics.signature import find_signature, plot_signature_performance, plot_selection_frequency

result = find_signature(two, "diabetes", positive="T2DM", covariates=["sex"])
```

```text
--8<-- "outputs/pad_find_signature.txt"
```

<figcaption markdown>`plot_signature_performance(result)`. The ROC curve of logistic regression and random forest. Line: the mean over the 5 repeats; band: their range. The dashed diagonal is a coin toss.</figcaption>

<figure class="sg-chart" markdown>
<div class="sg-plot" data-src="../../assets/figures/selection_frequency.json"><img src="../../assets/figures/selection_frequency.svg" alt="How often each protein was chosen across the 25 folds" loading="lazy"></div>
<figcaption markdown>`plot_selection_frequency(result)`. Flt3L and IL8 are chosen in 22 of the 25 folds.</figcaption>
</figure>

The proteins the group comparison found come back: Flt3L, IL8 and CDCP1, with TWEAK and HGF.
They are chosen often, so the signal in them is steady. Yet with sex in the model the proteins
reach an AUC of 0.65, and sex alone reaches 0.68: in this study the proteins add nothing that can
be shown on top of sex. This agrees with the adjusted comparison in step 8. An honest answer
for these 117 people: an inflammation panel alone does not predict T2DM well, and a larger study
with fuller sample data would be needed to separate the proteins from sex.
