# Obesity and diabetes (PAD000040)

Every step on real data: two raw plate files from a free public dataset, to quality-checked,
bridged data with its sample metadata. Every output is real.
{ .lede }

## 1. Get the data

**PAD000040** from the PRIDE archive (free to use, CC0): Olink Target 96 Inflammation, 92 proteins,
142 people with severe obesity, two plates, diabetes status. Download three files from
[PRIDE: PAD000040](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/pride/archive/projects/PAD000040) into one folder:

| File | What it is |
|---|---|
| `inflam_NASH_dt1.raw.csv` | plate 1, raw |
| `inflam_PRA_dt2.raw.csv` | plate 2, raw |
| `2025.05.08_sample_manifest.xlsx` | the sample list, with diabetes status |

<p class="small">Milito MM, …, Giraudi PJ. Inflammatory proteomic heterogeneity beyond glycemia
status in severe obesity. Int J Mol Sci 2026;27(9):4152.
<a href="https://doi.org/10.3390/ijms27094152">doi:10.3390/ijms27094152</a></p>

## 2. Read the two plates

```python
import pandas as pd
import signatomics.olink as so

plates = pd.concat([so.read_npx("inflam_NASH_dt1.raw.csv"),
                    so.read_npx("inflam_PRA_dt2.raw.csv")], ignore_index=True)
```

The reading report of plate 2:

```text
Reading report
  file: inflam_PRA_dt2.raw.csv
  file type: .csv
  layout: long format (one row per sample and assay)
  columns renamed to the names signatomics uses: PlateLOD -> LOD
  NPX: 184 cells said No Data, read as missing values
  MissingFreq (missing frequency): percentages ("12%") read as fractions (0.12)
  result: 8096 rows = 88 samples x 92 assays
```

The result is the [standard table](../functions/index.md#the-standard-table): one row per sample
and assay.

## 3. Quality control

The diabetes status is in the sample list, so the quality check looks it up there:

```python
manifest = "2025.05.08_sample_manifest.xlsx"
so.qc_report(plates, group="diabetes", samples=manifest, on="FIF ID", show_ids=False)
```

```text
Quality report
  data: 178 measurements of 143 samples, 92 assays, 2 plates
  QC status: 161 PASS, 17 WARN, 0 FAIL
  WARNING empty measurements (no NPX value at all): 2 (IDs hidden)
  bridge samples (on more than one plate): 35
  assays below the LOD in more than 75% of the samples: 8 of 92 (a sign of data quality; they stay in)
  outliers: 1 of 178 measurements (IDs hidden); see plot_sample_qc
  WARNING group mixed up with plate: 'Prediabetes' is missing from Inflammation F.I.F 21/03/2024. A plate difference can then look like a group difference: bridge the plates before comparing groups.
```

Two findings matter here. People with prediabetes were measured only on plate 2 (or on both), so
a plate difference would look like a diabetes difference: bridge before comparing.

```python
clean = so.apply_qc(plates, show_ids=False)
```

```text
Quality steps applied
  removed 2 measurements (no NPX value at all): 2 (IDs hidden)
  kept: 176 measurements of 143 samples, 92 assays
```

Every protein stays unless you give a threshold (`max_below_lod=`): a protein undetectable in
one group and measurable in another can itself be a biomarker. To remove the odd measurement as
well, see [Quality control](../learn/quality-control.md).

## 4. Bridging

```python
so.bridge_check(clean, group="diabetes", samples=manifest, on="FIF ID", show_ids=False)
```

```text
Bridging check
  reference: Inflammation F.I.F 21/03/2024
  Inflammation PRAESIIDIUM Giraudi 04_03_2025: reads +0.52 NPX compared with Inflammation F.I.F 21/03/2024 (median over assays; per assay -0.96 to +2.30), measured on 29 bridge samples
    assays shifted by more than 0.1 NPX: 83 of 92 - bridging needed
    noise: the bridge samples' differences spread 0.44 NPX per assay (typical IQR); bridging removes the shift; this noise stays
    bridge samples that shift differently from the others (a mix-up or a degraded tube?): 2 (IDs hidden)
  'diabetes' among the bridge samples: No diabetes: 9 of 69; T2DM: 9 of 48; Prediabetes: 10 of 25
    overall shift on Inflammation PRAESIIDIUM Giraudi 04_03_2025 per group: No diabetes +0.56; Prediabetes +0.57; T2DM +0.53 (largest difference 0.04 NPX)
    assays whose shift differs by more than 0.5 NPX between groups: IL13, IL-22 RA1, TNFB, 4E-BP1, IL-24, IL2 - with few bridge samples per group this is often noise; look at them before trusting group differences for these assays
```

Plate 2 differs from plate 1 by −0.96 to +2.30 NPX depending on the protein, so each protein gets
its own correction. Overall the shift is the same in every diabetes group. Six assays shift
differently between groups, each measured on only 9 or 10 bridge samples per group: look at
those before trusting a diabetes difference in them.

## 5. Add the sample metadata

```python
data = so.add_sample_info(bridged, manifest, on="FIF ID")
```

```text
Matching report
  matched on: SampleID (NPX data) = FIF ID (your sample metadata)
  matched: 142 of 143 sample IDs in the NPX data
  WARNING missing from your sample metadata (kept, without sample metadata): 249
  columns in both tables (yours renamed with the ending _samplelist): PlateID
```

`on` names the ID column in the sample list. Sample 249 has no row there: it stays in the data,
listed in the report.

## 6. Analysis table

For the statistics, run the route once more from the plates and remove sample 249 on plate 2, the
broken tube from the bridging step, before bridging. Then make the analysis table: one row per
person, one column per assay. The bridge samples were measured twice; `to_wide` averages their two
values.

```python
data = so.add_sample_info(plates, manifest, on="FIF ID")
clean = so.apply_qc(data, drop_samples={"249": "Inflammation PRAESIIDIUM Giraudi 04_03_2025"})
table = so.to_wide(so.bridge_plates(clean))
```

```text
Analysis table
  averaged: 32 samples measured more than once (bridge samples or repeats) get the average of their NPX values
  result: 143 samples x 92 assays, plus 10 columns of sample metadata
```

## 7. Compare the groups

The groups run from no diabetes through prediabetes to type 2 diabetes (T2DM), so one trend test
asks the sharpest question: does an assay rise step by step?

```python
from signatomics.stats import compare_groups, plot_volcano

order = ["No diabetes", "Prediabetes", "T2DM"]
trend = compare_groups(table, "diabetes", reference="No diabetes", ordered=True, order=order)
```

```text
Group comparison
  test: linear trend over the group steps (robust HC3 standard errors); order: No diabetes < Prediabetes < T2DM
  groups: No diabetes 69, Prediabetes 25, T2DM 48 (reference: No diabetes)
  left out: 1 sample without a value in 'diabetes'
  tested: 92 proteins
  FDR below 0.05: 3 proteins: IL8 (+0.30 NPX, FDR 0.016), Flt3L (+0.23 NPX, FDR 0.016), CDCP1 (+0.23 NPX, FDR 0.033)
```

IL8, Flt3L and CDCP1 rise by 0.2 to 0.3 NPX per step, so T2DM sits about 0.5 NPX (1.4 times) above
no diabetes.

<figure class="sg-chart" markdown>
<div class="sg-plot" data-src="../../assets/figures/volcano_trend.json"></div>
<figcaption markdown>`plot_volcano(trend)`. Each dot is one assay; the three coloured dots pass the FDR of 0.05.</figcaption>
</figure>

Without an order, `compare_groups(table, "diabetes", reference="No diabetes")` runs Welch's ANOVA, which
accepts any pattern and finds no assay at an FDR of 0.05 (lowest FDR 0.09). The trend test puts its
power on one pattern. The choice between them is made before looking at the results. The pairs in
`.pairwise` show that T2DM differs from no diabetes for all three assays (Holm p of 0.001 to
0.004), with prediabetes in between.

## 8. Adjust for covariates

The sample list holds one covariate, sex, and it is unevenly spread: 62 of the 69 people without
diabetes are women, against 26 of the 48 with T2DM. `covariates=["sex"]` fits `NPX ~ diabetes + sex`
for every assay, with the same model for all of them.

```python
from signatomics.stats import plot_volcano_pair

two = table[table["diabetes"].isin(["No diabetes", "T2DM"])]
unadjusted = compare_groups(two, "diabetes", reference="No diabetes")
adjusted = compare_groups(two, "diabetes", reference="No diabetes", covariates=["sex"])
plot_volcano_pair(unadjusted, adjusted)
```

<figure class="sg-chart" markdown>
<div class="sg-plot" data-src="../../assets/figures/volcano_pair.json"></div>
<figcaption markdown>`plot_volcano_pair(unadjusted, adjusted)`. Left: three assays pass the FDR. Right: after adjusting for sex none does, and the direction stays the same.</figcaption>
</figure>

Unadjusted, Flt3L, IL8 and CDCP1 pass an FDR of 0.05. After adjusting for sex the differences shrink
by 18 to 32% (IL8 from +0.60 to +0.40 NPX) and the intervals widen, and no assay passes. Only 7 people
without diabetes are men, so the data separate "T2DM" from "male" poorly. What the data support: a
difference of about +0.4 to +0.6 NPX with T2DM, and this study is too small to say how much of it sex
explains. A sample list with age, BMI and medication would give a fuller adjusted analysis with the
same two lines.

## 9. Find a signature

Can a few proteins tell T2DM from no diabetes in people the model has not seen? Sex goes into
every model, so the report also shows what sex alone predicts.

```python
from signatomics.signature import find_signature, plot_signature_performance, plot_selection_frequency

result = find_signature(two, "diabetes", positive="T2DM", covariates=["sex"])
```

```text
Biomarker signature
  predicting 'diabetes' = T2DM (binary)
  people: 117 (48 T2DM, 69 No diabetes); 92 proteins
  method: nested cross-validation, 5 folds x 5 repeats = 25 folds; in every training part an L1 model (penalty chosen by inner cross-validation, the one-standard-error rule) chooses the proteins; sex enters every model and is never dropped
  linear: AUC 0.65 (95% interval 0.56 to 0.74)
  forest: AUC 0.62 (95% interval 0.51 to 0.73)
  linear (covariates only): AUC 0.68 (95% interval 0.61 to 0.74)
  forest (covariates only): AUC 0.68 (95% interval 0.61 to 0.74)
  signature: 5 proteins chosen in at least 50% of the folds: Flt3L (88%), IL8 (88%), CDCP1 (64%), TWEAK (60%), HGF (52%)
  the performance above comes from people left out of every step that chose proteins; the final model (.predict) is fitted on everyone
```

<div class="sg-plot" data-src="../../assets/figures/signature_performance.json"><img src="../../assets/figures/signature_performance.svg" alt="ROC curves for T2DM: close to the diagonal" loading="lazy"></div>
<figcaption markdown>`plot_signature_performance(result)`. One line per repeat of the cross-validation; the dashed diagonal is a coin toss.</figcaption>

<figure class="sg-chart" markdown>
<div class="sg-plot" data-src="../../assets/figures/selection_frequency.json"><img src="../../assets/figures/selection_frequency.svg" alt="How often each protein was chosen across the 25 folds" loading="lazy"></div>
<figcaption markdown>`plot_selection_frequency(result)`. Flt3L and IL8 are chosen in 22 of the 25 folds.</figcaption>

The proteins the group comparison found come back: Flt3L, IL8 and CDCP1, with TWEAK and HGF.
They are chosen often, so the signal in them is steady. Yet with sex in the model the proteins
reach an AUC of 0.65, and sex alone reaches 0.68: in this study the proteins add nothing that can
be shown on top of sex. This agrees with the adjusted comparison in step 8. An honest answer
for these 117 people: an inflammation panel alone does not predict T2DM well, and a larger study
with fuller sample data would be needed to separate the proteins from sex.
